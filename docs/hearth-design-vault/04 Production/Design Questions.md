# Design Questions

> [[00 Start Here|Home]] › [[01 Design Guide|Design Guide]] › **04 Production** · Next: [[Prototype and Vertical Slices]]

This is the central index of questions that may need answers. The order is useful but not binding: questions can be answered, revisited, reordered, added, or removed at any time.

The **Current answer summary** is the project's present answer based only on accepted decisions. It is not a summary of possible directions or material to explore. When discussion changes or completes an answer, overwrite that cell. If no answer has been accepted, write **Not decided**.

Detailed reasoning and alternatives belong in the linked exploration note.

## Questions and current answers

|   # | Question                                                                 | Current answer summary                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Exploration                                                                             |
| --: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|   1 | **What is the premise and larger story of the world?**                   | In a lush magic-fantasy world, the player characters have left a homeland ravaged by war and arrived together in a large city. They begin with little money or reputation and try to build new lives and make names for themselves through different paths. | [[World Premise]]                                                                       |
|   2 | **How does the player character progress?**                              | Separate mastery tracks unlock options, recipes, convenience, and expression. Gear is situational, builds are freely changeable at home, and power has a reachable cap so newcomers remain useful.                                                          | [[Player Progression]]                                                                  |
|   3 | How does the shared world progress?                                      | Shared projects improve the homestead and settlements; major outcomes change regions and enter a shared chronicle. Progress expands choices and never decays offline.                                                                                       | [[Shared World Progression]]                                                            |
|   4 | Who is the player in this world, and what is their central fantasy?      | An ordinary newcomer becomes known for a chosen life and leaves a visible mark on people, places, and the group's history.                                                                                                                                  | [[Player Fantasy]]                                                                      |
|   5 | What does a player repeatedly do during a normal session?                | Choose a goal, venture out, return to turn gains into home or reputation improvements, and leave a small persistent trace.                                                                                                                                  | [[Core Play Loop]]                                                                      |
|   6 | What kind of world is it physically?                                     | A large finite 3D continent: authored city and major sites surrounded by seeded, streamed procedural regions.                                                                                                                                               | [[World Shape and Building#Physical world]]                                             |
|   7 | How much can players build, reshape, or destroy?                         | Players build freely within claimed plots using modular pieces and limited terrain shaping. Cities and landmarks cannot be destroyed.                                                                                                                       | [[World Shape and Building#Building and destruction]]                                   |
|   8 | What makes the world feel alive rather than empty?                       | NPC routines and relationships, settlement conditions, a context-aware event director, and persistent memories create readable local change. A persistent authoritative clock drives readable routines and bounded safe calendar catch-up. | [[Living World]]                                                                        |
|   9 | What is fun when playing completely alone?                               | Solo players can scout, gather, build, farm, craft, trade, and follow personal stories; group danger can wait or use NPC help.                                                                                                                              | [[Solo and Cooperative Play#Solo]]                                                      |
|  10 | What becomes different—not merely easier—when friends join?              | Friends take complementary roles, solve simultaneous tasks, make shared choices, rescue one another, and create social stories.                                                                                                                             | [[Solo and Cooperative Play#What friends change]]                                       |
|  11 | How should play scale from two to eight players?                         | Encounters add varied threats and parallel jobs; loot is personal, project credit is shared, and drop-in/out does not reset outings.                                                                                                                        | [[Solo and Cooperative Play#Scaling from two to eight]]                                 |
|  12 | How does exploration work?                                               | Rumors, roads, viewpoints, and landmarks reveal a shared map. First journeys are eventful; routes and waypoints make repeats faster.                                                                                                                        | [[Exploration and Combat#Exploration]]                                                  |
|  13 | How does combat work, and how important is it?                           | Accessible action in either first person or close over-the-shoulder third person uses a small swappable loadout and rewards positioning and cooperation. Combat matters in danger but is not required for every life path.                                   | [[Exploration and Combat#Combat]]                                                       |
|  14 | How do gathering, farming, crafting, building, and management connect?   | Sources feed simple production into supplies, tools, trade, decoration, and shared projects; settlement needs make outputs matter.                                                                                                                          | [[Life Skills and Economy#Connected activities]]                                        |
|  15 | How do quests, local stories, NPCs, factions, and world events work?     | Authored characters and arcs combine with events generated from real local conditions. Outcomes alter relationships, access, places, and the chronicle.                                                                                                     | [[Stories and Consequences#Stories, NPCs, factions, and events]]                        |
|  16 | What happens when players fail, die, or lose something?                  | Players can be revived or return to safety, leaving recoverable gathered materials but never gear or permanent progress. Failure changes situations and creates recovery stories.                                                                           | [[Stories and Consequences#Failure and death]]                                          |
|  17 | How do loot, equipment, resources, trading, and the economy work?        | Loot emphasizes options and expression. Players trade directly; bounded local markets, useful services, projects, and decoration keep coin circulating.                                                                                                     | [[Life Skills and Economy#Loot, trade, and currency]]                                   |
|  18 | How does optional PvP work?                                              | PvP exists only in opt-in contests. Standard play normalizes power; chaos mode may use personal gear. Rewards do not grant exclusive power.                                                                                                                 | [[PvP and Party Activities#Optional PvP]]                                               |
|  19 | What happens while players are offline?                                  | The world sleeps when empty and applies safe, bounded catch-up on return. Absence never causes destruction or decay; online changes are recorded for the group.                                                                                             | [[Stories and Consequences#While players are offline]]                                  |
|  20 | What session lengths and rhythms should the game support?                | Useful play fits 10–20 minutes, outings fit 30–60, and major adventures fit 60–120, with safe exits and checkpoints.                                                                                                                                        | [[Session and Presentation#Session rhythm]]                                             |
|  21 | What camera, controls, presentation, and visual style fit the game?      | An inviting stylized fantasy world uses atmospheric lighting and organic modular environments, simplified modest humanoids rather than realistic or Minecraft-like bodies, and a beautiful low-noise UI with smooth feedback. First person remains default with close over-the-shoulder third person; controls are controller-first and touch adapts contextually. | [[Session and Presentation#Camera, controls, and style]]                                |
|  22 | What role do party activities or minigames have?                         | Short contests and festivals occur at real world locations and use the same characters and rewards; they are social breaks, not a separate game.                                                                                                            | [[PvP and Party Activities#Party activities]]                                           |
|  23 | What should Discord add?                                                 | Nothing required at launch. Later it may add presence, invites, and opt-in notices, but no gameplay, command grinding, or exclusive rewards.                                                                                                                | [[Discord]]                                                                             |
|  24 | What is the smallest prototype that can prove the game is fun?           | “A New Home”: 1–4 players help one resident, explore a small forest, overcome one obstacle, repair a cottage, and return to a world that remembers it.                                                                                                      | [[First Vertical Slice]]                                                                |
|  25 | What platforms, networking, persistence, and server model are realistic? | Launch targets are Windows, desktop browser, and native Android. The same authoritative rules support device-local offline worlds, native LAN-hosted games, and dedicated WebSocket rooms. Their saves stay separate; browsers can play offline or join but cannot host. | [[Technical and Business Research#25. Platforms, networking, persistence, and servers]] |
|  26 | How is the game sold and supported after launch?                         | Price is not decided. Test slices are free; later pricing follows demonstrated player value, not development hours. Steam is an optional later Windows channel, while itch.io or the project website supports early distribution.                           | [[Technical and Business Research#26. Business model and post-launch]]                  |
|  27 | What engine and technical structure should be used?                      | Use stable Godot 4.x with GDScript and the Compatibility renderer. One authoritative simulation runs locally for offline play or behind the shared WebSocket protocol for LAN and dedicated rooms; every slice must pass on all three targets. | [[Technical and Business Research#27. Engine and technical structure]]                  |
|  28 | How is the game divided into playable vertical slices?                   | Build six cumulative slices after a multiplayer proof: a new home, living world, exploration, chosen livelihoods, full shared-world scale, then social activities and launch polish.                                                                        | [[Prototype and Vertical Slices#Playable slices]]                                       |
|  29 | How should AI-generated assets be used and disclosed?                    | Keep provenance and commercial-rights records, review and edit every asset, disclose material AI use, avoid imitation or recognizable protected material, and exclude live generation initially.                                                            | [[AI Asset Policy]]                                                                     |
