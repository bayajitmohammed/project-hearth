# Life Skills and Economy

> [[00 Start Here|Home]] › [[01 Design Guide|Design Guide]] › **03 Progression and Activities** · Previous: [[Shared World Progression]] · Next: [[PvP and Party Activities]]

## Connected activities

Gathering, farming, fishing, and exploration provide materials. Cooking and crafting turn them into expedition supplies, tools, trade goods, decorations, and project contributions. Building creates useful spaces that improve those activities. Settlement needs provide changing reasons to produce different goods.

Each step should make a visible difference; the game avoids long processing chains and inventory micromanagement.

## Renewable forest forage and trailcraft

After the cottage is repaired, the five authored forest wood and herb nodes renew together at the start of each authoritative world day. Gathered materials remain in the shared project bag so companions can forage in parallel or leave ingredients for whoever returns home first. A missed or empty day never stacks extra materials: calendar catch-up may make the current day's nodes available, but it does not gather them.

At the cottage **trailwork bench**, a nearby standing player may spend one shared wood and one shared herb to make one personal trail provision. The original repair-kit recipe keeps priority while the cottage tutorial is active. Trailcraft grants no mastery, coin, reputation, or shared-project credit; it simply gives gathering and crafting a renewable preparation purpose in offline, LAN-hosted, and dedicated worlds. Each craft conserves its inputs and output, so friends may divide foraging and preparation without creating passive supplies.

## First fishing loop

Repairing the cottage makes its simple shared fishing gear available at **Willowmere Pond**. Each standing player may cast independently. The authoritative world provides a short wait followed by a clearly named one-second bite window; reeling early ends that cast without a catch, while missing the window lets the fish go and returns the player to ready. Cast timing is transient, never advances while the room is empty, and one player's cast cannot consume or reset another's.

Reeling during the bite grants one persistent personal **riverfish** and one Fishing mastery point. At the existing cottage cookfire, one personal riverfish becomes one personal trail provision and grants one Cooking mastery point. Cooking a required shared stew has priority whenever that action is currently valid, so fishing cannot accidentally divert project ingredients or inputs. Riverfish cannot be sold in this first loop and grants no coin, passive income, shared-project credit, or exclusive power. This is the first renewable chill activity that converts personal practice into useful outing preparation in offline, LAN-hosted, and dedicated worlds alike.

The first narrow shared storage is an eight-fish **riverfish creel** beside the repaired cottage. A nearby standing player may store one personal riverfish per interaction while space remains. Required hearth stew still has first priority at the cookfire; afterward, the cook uses a personal riverfish when carrying one and otherwise draws one conserved fish from the creel. Either source produces one personal trail provision and normal Cooking mastery for the cook. Storing fish grants no additional mastery, coin, or project credit, and stock never grows or disappears while the room is empty. This lets friends prepare ingredients asynchronously without introducing a general remote inventory or duplicating goods.

## Renewable neighborhood food orders

Opening the produce stall remains a one-time shared milestone. Beginning with the next in-game day, the authoritative world posts one repeatable neighborhood market request and regrows the four shared moonroot plots. The shared forecast provides the first readable market condition: clear days request three fresh moonroot, gentle-rain days request two warming hearth stews, and overcast days alternate between the two by day parity. Fresh-produce days emphasize farming and trade; stew days use the full garden → cookfire → market loop and create a reason to cook.

Farming, Cooking, and Trade mastery still belongs to the player who performs each step. Completing the daily request adds one provision to the bounded shared pantry, but does not repeatedly grant reputation, morale, or chronicle entries. If several days pass while the room is empty, only the current day's request is available; unattended time never completes requests or multiplies rewards. Festival activities remain available alongside the request rather than being replaced by it.

## Loot, trade, and currency

Loot favors recipes, unusual materials, tools with trade-offs, keepsakes, and cosmetics over constant numeric upgrades. Players can trade directly and contribute to shared storage.

Local NPC markets use bounded supply and demand. Coin pays for services, land, travel, and common goods; reputation unlocks access but is not spent. There is no global auction house. Repairs, projects, services, and decoration keep money useful without punishing upkeep.

The first player-to-player exchange is a one-item trail-provision handoff between nearby standing friends. It is a gift rather than a priced trade: ownership moves immediately and conservatively, with no tax, fee, mastery, coin, shared credit, or unattended transfer. Revive, field aid, and nearby world interactions retain priority. The cottage riverfish creel separately proves one bounded shared ingredient store for asynchronous cooperation. Broader item selection, negotiated trades, general shared storage, and remote exchange remain future systems.

The first coin loop begins only once the produce stall is open. Every unit a player personally delivers toward the milestone or a renewable market request pays that player one persistent coin; a Trade-II bulk delivery pays once per accepted unit. A separate supply basket sells one personal trail provision for two coins. It opens with three shared units and resets to three on each new world day; missed days never stack extra stock, and an empty room does not transact. Buyers share the remaining supply on a first-completed authoritative basis. Free shared-pantry pickup remains a distinct interaction and takes priority when stock exists. Coin belongs to the player, is never dropped on defeat, does not grow while the world is empty, and grants no power until deliberately spent. This proves earning, bounded local supply, and a practical common-goods sink without introducing price speculation, passive income, negotiated player trade, or a global market.

The first shared coin sink is the four-coin Hearthbloom planter beside the cottage. Contributions move one coin at a time from the contributing player into persistent shared project progress. The finished planter is a permanent visible neighborhood improvement with one-time morale, reputation, and chronicle recognition, not a source of power or recurring income. Players choose independently between personal supplies and the shared project, and both solo and cooperative worlds use the same costs and authority.
